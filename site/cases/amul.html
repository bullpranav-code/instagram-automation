Amul: the girl who kept a brand talking for 50 years

Accent market case study · 4 November 2026 · 4 min read

In 1966, Amul butter had been on the market for about ten years. Ad-man Sylvester Dacunha and art director Eustace Fernandes created a small girl in a polka-dot dress, and the line "Utterly Butterly Delicious" was born. Dr V. Kurien, who led Amul, thought the idea was "utterly mad" but let the team go ahead.

What made it work

  • Topical ads: the hoardings reacted to the news of the day, so people looked forward to the next one.
  • Freedom to move fast: Kurien let the team publish without waiting for his approval, and stood by them when some ads drew complaints.
  • Consistency: the same character, style and humour for about five decades, across more than 4,000 hoardings.

Lessons for marketers and sellers

  1. Be timely. Connect your message to what people are already talking about.
  2. Be consistent. One recognisable style builds memory and trust.
  3. Trust your team. Speed needs leaders who give permission and take responsibility.
