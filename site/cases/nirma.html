Nirma: how a ₹3 detergent beat the giants

Accent market case study · 4 November 2026 · 4 min read

In 1969, Karsanbhai Patel, a 24-year-old government lab employee in Gujarat, started making a phosphate-free detergent powder in his backyard. He named it Nirma, after his daughter Nirupama, and sold it while cycling to work.

What he did

  • Priced for the people: Nirma sold at about ₹3 per kg, roughly one-third of the cheapest popular detergent of the time.
  • Removed the risk: he offered a money-back guarantee, so trying it cost the customer nothing.
  • Spoke the customer's language: simple, household-friendly advertising and a jingle people remembered.

The result

Nirma created a new low-cost detergent segment in India. In the 1980s it overtook Surf in market share, and larger companies responded with cheaper products of their own.

Lessons for sales learners

  1. Know your customer's budget. Nirma won by fitting what most families could pay.
  2. Reduce the fear of saying yes. A guarantee turns "maybe" into "let me try".
  3. Start small, sell personally. Patel sold himself before he had a sales team.
